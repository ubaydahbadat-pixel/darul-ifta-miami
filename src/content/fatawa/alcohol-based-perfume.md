title: Alcohol-based perfume
category: hazr-ibahah
topic: Perfume (ʿIṭr)
date: '2026-10-30'
draft: true
question: Assalāmu ʿalaykum, is it okay to use designer perfumes from the high street?
short_answer: It is permissible to wear designer perfumes. The ethanol element found in perfumes is not impure or prohibited to use.
explanation:
- heading: The two types of alcohol
  text: |-
    Imām Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ (d. 370 AH) divides alcohols into two types:

    1. Khamr: that which is derived from dates and grapes.
    2. Non-khamr: that which is derived from everything other than dates and grapes.

    Imām Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ states that the alcohol derived from khamr is clearly ḥarām in small and large quantities, as has been clarified by the beloved Messenger ﷺ. Other types of alcohol are not included in this prohibition. Therefore, if they are found in small quantities, they will be permissible to use. However, Imām Abū Bakr al-Jaṣṣāṣ further states that if the non-khamr alcohol is found in a quantity large enough to intoxicate an individual, it too will not be allowed to be consumed. This explanation is further expanded upon by Imām Ibn ʿĀbidīn (d. 1252 AH) and other Ḥanafī jurists.
- heading: The alcohols found in perfume
  text: |-
    Perfumes typically contain three types of alcohol:

    1. Ethanol
    2. Isopropyl myristate
    3. Monopropylene glycol

    Ethanol is produced either synthetically or as a crop by-product. Isopropyl myristate and monopropylene glycol, without any organic components, are both by-products of petroleum and propane. As none of them are derived from grape or date sources (khamr sources), it would be acceptable to use them in perfumes.
- heading: Purity of the alcohol in perfume
  text: Muftī Taqī ʿUthmānī (ḥafiẓahullāh) further clarifies this discussion by stating that alcohol found in perfumes will be considered ṭāhir (pure) and not najis (impure), as it is not derived from grapes or dates. Muftī Taqī ʿUthmānī (ḥafiẓahullāh) further states that most of these types of alcohol are made from synthetic sources.
hikmah: The majority of consumer products use alcohols, including ethanol, although they are neither used nor intended as intoxicants. Rather, the purpose of these kinds of alcohols is to provide the final product with a chemical foundation. The khamr which the Almighty forbade was consumed with the intention of intoxication. Instead of making one drunk, the alcohol used in perfume works to make the mixture smell more aromatic.
